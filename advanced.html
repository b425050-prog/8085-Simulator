Model notes & source manuals
Instruction-level, deterministic simulation
All documented 8085 instructions, five condition flags, stack, 64 KB address space, interrupt priority, RIM/SIM, SID/SOD, two independent 8255s with modes 0/1/2 and BSR. Execution time uses Intel 8085 T-state counts. Bus and peripheral events occur at instruction boundaries.
ADC models use ideal 8-bit quantization with configurable conversion time; input is sampled at conversion start. DAC models expose ideal current and an external I/V stage. Analog settling, electrical loading and transistor behavior are outside this model. Undocumented opcodes stop with a diagnostic.
Scope capture: 32,768 samples. Traces: 2,048 transactions. High-frequency signals can alias; choose a suitable capture rate. The UI remains responsive when a program loops indefinitely.

WELCOME TO YOUR LAB
From assembly to a real signal.
Your entire lab runs locally. No installation, account, or internet connection is needed after launch.
  1. Enter your six-digit ID. The separate LED board displays two packed BCD digits on each port: PA, PB and PC.
  2. Assemble & load. The editor builds into user RAM at 2000H. Click line numbers for breakpoints.
  3. Run the experiment. Watch registers, LEDs and converters respond. Use Step to inspect each instruction.
  4. Measure it. Open Scope for two analog channels, eight digital lanes, triggering and cursors.
Memory is yours from 2000H to FFFFH.The internal monitor lives below 2000H. The two 8255s default to I/O addresses 80H–83H and 90H–93H.
